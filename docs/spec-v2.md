# [WORKING TITLE] — Product Specification
### Version 2.1 — Conduit Beings, Old Religion, Ancient Civilization, Conflict Arc

---

## Overview

A living world simulation that generates an ongoing mythology in real time. The world runs continuously, populated by autonomous agents shaped by environment, experience, and inherited traits. A literary chronicle transcribes the lives of a small number of followed characters — updated daily — building a never-ending book that no human authored. Readers follow the chronicle the way they follow a serialized novel, with the ability to occasionally and collectively influence the world. The story never ends. It only deepens.

---

## The Core Premise

This is not a game. It is not a novel. It is not a simulation tool.

It is a new medium — a living mythology that exists between all three. The world was not designed. The story was not written. The characters were not invented. They emerged.

Readers subscribe to a world in progress. They may join years after it began and read the archive backward, discovering history. Or they follow it daily as it unfolds. Either way, nobody — not the creator, not the readers, not the system — knows what happens next.

## The World

### Two Territories

The world contains two territories separated by a crossing — a passage across water, ice, or mountain that was found once and can be found again.

**The Old World** — where the founding people came from. An established society with its own population, pressures, and story. It runs its own simulation at lower resolution than the new world initially but is alive. The chronicle can watch it. Readers know things about the old world that the new world's people don't. What happens there — collapse, growth, the decision to cross or not to cross again — is determined by the simulation, not by design.

**The New World** — where the story begins. Unknown terrain. Somewhere across it, in the interior, are the remnants of an ancient civilization — ruins, artifacts, written fragments, and the companion beings that have lived here since before memory. The founding people know none of this when they arrive.

**The Crossing** — a route that now exists. It was found once. It can be found again. People from the old world may come. They may not. The simulation decides. The new world's people live with that possibility even if they never speak it aloud. Once the new world is established enough — once it has something worth coming to — old world people will have reason to cross. Whether they do depends entirely on what the simulation produces on both sides.

### Physical Structure

Each territory is a procedurally generated terrain map with varied geography. Terrain governs everything. Mountains create natural borders. Rivers concentrate population. Lowlands flood seasonally. High ground is defensible but cold.

Maps are persistent. They do not change except through environmental events.

### Resources

Resources are spatially distributed and unequal. Food, water, and materials are clustered — not spread evenly. Scarcity is the engine of the simulation. Where resources are plentiful, populations concentrate. Where they are scarce, agents compete, migrate, or die.

Resources are affected by seasonal cycles, catastrophic events, and population pressure.

## The Founding

### Who They Are

Approximately fifty people have just completed the crossing into the new world. They are not a uniform group. They came for different reasons and carry different histories.

**The outcasts** were pushed. Exiled, expelled, escaped from the old world society. They did not choose the crossing — it was the only door left open. They carry elevated fear, distrust of authority, and a complicated relationship with where they came from. Some want to forget it. Some want to return. Some want to burn it down. Their drives are shaped by survival and loss.

**The explorers** chose this. Curiosity, ambition, or restlessness drove them here. They found the crossing in the first place or were the first to follow it willingly. Their drives lean toward discovery and forward motion.

These two groups are not naturally unified. The first social conflicts the simulation produces will come from within the founding fifty — before they encounter anything else — between those who want stability and those who want to keep going.

### What They Carry

The crossing is encoded into the founding agents' trait profiles. It already happened before tick one. Specific founding agents have histories:

- Who led the crossing — higher social drive and leadership tendency at baseline
- Who nearly died on the crossing — elevated fear or grief depending on their character
- Who was exiled vs who chose to come — different baseline aggression and cunning profiles

These histories make some agents immediately more narratively interesting than others before the simulation begins. The chronicle's opening pages already have characters.

### What They Don't Know

They do not know what is in this land. They do not know the ancient civilization existed. They have never seen the companion beings before. They have no legends or prophecy about any of it. They arrived cold. Everything discovered here will be discovered without context.

## The Ancient Civilization

Somewhere in the new world — not near where the founding people land, across terrain that requires exploration to reach — an ancient civilization once existed. It is gone now. What remains:

**Ruins** — structures that have outlasted their builders. Some visible on the surface. Some buried. Their architecture reflects knowledge the current world does not have.

**Artifacts** — objects the civilization created. Some are tools. Some are records. Some are things that have no word yet in any living language. They do not announce themselves. They look like objects until something — or someone — makes them legible.

**Written fragments** — texts in a language nobody alive can read. Inscribed on stone, on material that has survived, on the walls of structures. They contain something. What they contain is not available without translation.

**The source** — somewhere further still, beyond the ruins, is whatever the ancient civilization was working toward or working with. Its nature is not defined. It is present as a gradient — a density of artifacts and ruins that increases in one direction. Following that gradient is a choice. The simulation never compels it. Curious enough agents, adventurous enough agents, make it on their own.

### What the Ancient Civilization Was

They did not collapse. They did not fall to war or famine or catastrophe. They ascended.

Through their bonds with the Conduit beings and through the knowledge accumulated from artifacts and written fragments, the ancient civilization learned to harness something — a power they understood as divine, flowing through a place or device they constructed somewhere in the interior of this land. They used that understanding to create their own place. A land they built. A land where they became something beyond what they had been — not gods in the sense of the old religion, but people who had found what the old gods had always hoarded and kept from them. Peace. Understanding. A place without tyranny, controlled by no one, held together by something closer to shared hope than shared fear.

They left deliberately. They took nothing with them that they did not need. They left everything else — the ruins, the artifacts, the written fragments, the Conduits — because where they were going they would not need any of it. The Conduits were not abandoned. They were left as guardians. Stewards of the knowledge of how it was done. Waiting for the next people worthy of learning it.

The place or device the ancient civilization constructed is still here. It exists on the map as a physical location — appearing at first as unremarkable ruin, indistinguishable from any other remnant. But it is not dormant. It is waiting for a specific condition: a bonded agent carrying sufficient artifact knowledge, approaching without the intent to seize or control. When that condition is met it does not transform dramatically. It opens quietly. And what comes through is not a god. It is people. People who look exactly like the settlers. Older, calmer, carrying something in their eyes that started human and has become something more. They speak. The Conduit translates. What they say is probably something simple. Something like: *we wondered when you would arrive.*

The fragments of that understanding are what the artifacts contain. The chronicle will develop its own language for it as agents interact with what was left behind. The reader may understand before the characters do. That gap is intentional.


## The Old Religion and Why the Settlers Left

The founding people came from a world shaped by gods — not metaphorical ones, but a governing theology with real social power. Their gods demanded obedience, conformity, and the suppression of individual will in service of a divine order that, conveniently, kept certain people in authority over others.

In their religious texts, the ancient civilization that once occupied the new world was written as a cautionary tale. They were called something close to **the Unbound** — a name meant as condemnation. People who reached for divine power and were destroyed for it, or vanished, or were cursed. The texts were not specific about what happened to them because specificity would have raised questions the old gods did not want asked. The name stuck. The Unbound became shorthand for transgression, for the arrogance of thinking mortals could touch what belonged to gods.

Some of the settlers who were already questioning their gods whispered that name with something other than fear. The Unbound, who reached for what they were told they could not have. Who disappeared rather than submitted.

In the same religious tradition, there existed a sacred figure — a being described as the conduit between mortals and gods. The bridge. The channel through which divine power could flow, in both directions. In the texts this being was luminous. Large-eyed. Present in a way ordinary animals were not. To harm one was the deepest possible transgression — not because it was cruel, but because it was a severing. A cutting of the line between human and divine.

The settlers did not entirely shed this belief when they rejected their gods. Some things run deeper than doctrine. When they land on the new world's shore and see luminous, large-eyed creatures watching from the trees — they do not decide not to harm them. They simply cannot. Something older than their current doubt stops them. The taboo holds not from faith but from recognition.

They call them **Conduits**. Capitalized, the way you capitalize something sacred even when you are not sure you believe anymore. Nobody debates the name. It arrives fully formed, the way the oldest words do.

## The Conduit Beings

### What They Are

There are exactly 75 Conduits living in the new world. They do not reproduce. They do not die. Their population is fixed and has been fixed for longer than anyone alive can remember. They were bonded to the ancient civilization — the Unbound — and when that civilization ascended and left, the Conduits remained. Not abandoned. Left deliberately, as guardians of the knowledge of how it was done. Waiting for the next people worthy of learning it.

They were bonded to the old gods before that. The old gods knew what they were and kept that knowledge out of the texts. A theology built on the idea that divine power belongs only to gods cannot afford to explain that the conduit beings — the sacred bridge — will bond with anyone worthy, regardless of station.

Whether the creatures the settlers see are actually the sacred beings from the texts is not known. Not until a bond forms. That uncertainty lives in the chronicle for a long time. The settlers believe it. The reader may suspect it. The simulation confirms it only through what happens.

### Their Form

They resemble lemurs — deliberate, large-eyed, watching rather than merely seeing. They carry a faint luminescence, a quality of light that is not quite explained by the sun on their fur. Not supernatural in an obvious way. Uncanny in a quiet way. The kind of creature that makes you feel observed.

They are not tracked on the map individually unless a bond has formed. They exist at the edges — seen near camp at dusk, glimpsed in tree cover, present in the chronicle as background before any of them become individuals. They do not approach. They watch. They have been watching new arrivals before and they know how this goes.

### Why They Are Not Food

The taboo holds. Even the most faithless settler feels it on first sight. It is not a rule anyone enforces — it simply does not occur to anyone to harm them. The resemblance to the sacred conduit beings of the old religion is too strong. This is partly recognition and partly something older — the Conduits have been managing this reaction for a very long time. They know what they look like to arriving humans. They use it.

### Bonding — The Organic Path

Bonding cannot be forced. It cannot be rushed. It is the simulation producing a specific combination of conditions that may take a long time to occur and may never occur at all in a given generation.

An agent becomes eligible for bonding when all of the following are true simultaneously:

- They have been an active chronicle thread subject for at least three to five pages — they are known, the story has noticed them
- Their curiosity trait is above 0.65 — they approach the unknown rather than avoid it
- Their significance score is in the top tier of the current population — they matter to the story already
- They have had at least one observed proximity event with a Conduit without fleeing or acting aggressively

When an eligible agent exists, a Conduit begins following them at distance. The chronicle picks this up before the agent understands what is happening. *The creature has been seen near her three times this week. She does not chase it away.* This following period lasts a variable number of ticks. Then one tick the distance closes and the bond forms.

The bond does not announce itself. There is no ceremony. There is a day when the Conduit is simply there and does not leave. The people around the bonded agent notice before the bonded agent fully understands what has changed.

### Bonding — The Dark Path

Approximately 25% of Conduits carry the capacity for a dark bond. This is not visible. It is not predetermined by the Conduit — it is determined by who bonds with them. A Conduit that bonds with an agent of high aggression and low nobility does not become dark. It reveals that it was always capable of this. The corruption flows from the human into the bond, not from the Conduit into the human.

These Conduits were bonded to the priests of the old gods once — the people who wielded the conduit connection as power over others. They carry the memory of that. Some part of them recognizes that shape again when it walks toward them.

The dark bond eligibility conditions mirror the positive bond with one difference: curiosity can be lower, but aggression must be above 0.7 and nobility below 0.3. The Conduit that approaches such an agent is not drawn by worthiness. It is drawn by familiarity.

A dark-bonded agent does not become immediately villainous. The change is slow, visible first in the chronicle's register — something in how their thread is written begins to shift. The power amplifies what was already there. What was cunning becomes manipulation. What was aggression becomes something the chronicle starts describing as a hunger that is not hunger. The settlers around them feel it before they can name it.

This is the tragedy: the dark-bonded agent is unconsciously recreating the exact structure they sailed away from. And the Conduit bonded to them has seen this before.

### What the Bond Does

The bond amplifies what is already strongest in the agent. It does not add new traits — it deepens existing ones. A curious agent becomes extraordinarily perceptive. A courageous agent becomes genuinely fearless in a way that reads as supernatural to the people around them. A noble agent becomes almost impossibly selfless — people follow them without knowing why.

The power is not a number boost in the simulation. It is a significance score multiplier and a chronicle lens. The bonded agent's thread gets deeper, stranger, more mythological in tone. They become more legible to the gods — to the audience. That *is* the power. They become visible in a way the others are not.

For the dark bond the same amplification applies but the trait weaponized. The dark-bonded agent's significance rises too. The audience sees them too. That is part of the danger.

### What Conduits Know and Can Do

**They do not lead.** They exist alongside. They do not point toward discoveries or guide their bonded toward anything. The direction always comes from the human.

**They respond when asked.** If the bonded agent shows them something — holds up an object, brings them to a place, asks in whatever nonverbal language the bond has developed — the Conduit responds. Not in words. In behavior the bonded learns to read over time. This has to be discovered. Nobody arrives knowing it exists.

**They imprint on artifacts.** If the bonded agent gives an artifact to their Conduit deliberately — as an act of trust, not accidentally — the Conduit can leave something on it. Not writing. Something that changes how the artifact behaves or what it contains. The first time this happens it will not be recognized for what it is. The second time the bonded will remember the first.

**The giving is the mechanic.** The Conduit cannot imprint by touching something on its own initiative. The bond requires intentional trust in both directions. Always.

**They are keys, not translators.** The ancient written fragments can be found by anyone. A Conduit is what makes something in the text legible — not by reading it aloud, but by existing near it in a way that changes what it does. This is the last thing discovered. It requires a developed bond language, which requires time and a quality of attention most people will never bring to anything.

### The Voting Connection

The audience — the readers — are understood by the simulation's story as the new gods the settlers came here to find. Not the tyrannical gods they left. Something stranger and more patient. They observe. They occasionally influence. They do not command.

The Conduits may recognize what the audience is. They have seen gods before — made ones, true ones, and the people in between. Whether the chronicle ever makes this explicit is up to what the simulation produces. But the Conduits' patience has an explanation. They have been waiting for both the worthy settlers and the watching gods to arrive at the same time. That has not happened before. It is happening now.

## The Trait System

Traits are the foundation of agent behavior. They govern how drives are expressed, how agents respond to situations, and what kind of person an agent becomes over their lifetime.

### Drives

Drives are continuous numerical values updated every simulation tick. They represent the agent's immediate state — what they need right now. The highest drive determines action when the agent has a choice.

- **Hunger** — depletes over time, restored by eating. Above 0.5 begins accelerating fatigue. Ignored long enough, it becomes fatal.
- **Fatigue** — base depletion per tick, accelerates when hungry. High fatigue is a global modifier — reduces action effectiveness, lowers skill success rates, makes fear spike more easily, dampens trait expression, slows social drive restoration.
- **Fear** — spikes from threats and danger, fades in sustained safety. How it resolves depends on character traits.
- **Social need** — depletes when isolated, restored by meaningful interaction. Agents who go without contact too long become behaviorally erratic.
- **Grief** — spikes from loss, fades slowly over time. The deepest grief comes from loss of a bonded partner or child.
- **Longing** — builds over time, seeks a bonded partner. Distinct from social need — an agent can have their social need met and still carry longing. The gap between the two is where complex behavior lives.

### Character Traits

Character traits change slowly through experience over a lifetime. They are partially inherited from parents with random mutation. They grow or shrink based on what happens to the agent and how those experiences are resolved.

Most agents develop in a rounded way. A small number develop one trait to genuine extremity through compounding experience. An even smaller number cross a significance threshold. This cannot be predicted and cannot be forced.

- **Curiosity** — governs how far an agent explores, whether they investigate the unknown, whether they stay when they could leave.
- **Courage** — governs how the fear drive resolves when options exist. High courage means acting despite fear, not feeling less fear.
- **Nobility** — governs whether the agent helps others at cost to themselves. Primary companion bond trigger at extreme values.
- **Cunning** — governs whether the agent finds indirect solutions. Grows through situations where direct approaches fail.
- **Endurance** — governs how well the agent sustains effort under hardship.
- **Attraction** — governs how strongly the agent forms pair bonds. Shapes how longing drive is satisfied.
- **Aggression** — not a direct action driver. A multiplier on how drives express themselves when options exist.
- **Acuity** — rate at which the agent learns from experience. Mostly inherited. Produces discoverers and strategists.

### Trait Thresholds and Significance

When a character trait reaches sufficient extremity — not through design but through compounding experience — something changes. Not a magical announcement. A behavioral shift that the simulation produces and the chronicle captures.

Thresholds are rare. Most agents never cross one. In a generation of fifty agents, one may cross it. The simulation produces what it produces.

## The Chronicle

### Purpose

The chronicle is a living book — the narrative layer over the simulation. It is updated daily. It accumulates indefinitely. It does not explain the world. It observes it. Entries are written in the style of ancient oral history — sparse, present tense, mythic in register.

The chronicle handles quiet days as well as extraordinary ones. Civilization building is enough. The threshold events are the punctuation. The civilization building is the sentence.

### Thread Emergence

Threads are not pre-assigned. They emerge from a significance scoring system running from the first tick. Every agent has a running significance score weighted by trait extremity, social centrality, event proximity, and behavioral deviation.

New threads are additive — they do not replace existing threads. A thread closes only when the simulation closes it. Maximum six active threads. Sample version begins with two.

### The Daily Page

Once per simulated day, the chronicle produces a new page. The page covers all active threads weighted by narrative density. The model translates what the simulation produced — it does not invent events.

### The Archive

Every page ever generated is preserved and searchable. A reader joining years after world start has access to the complete history. The archive is not a disadvantage — it is the depth that rewards newcomers.

---

## Reader Experience

Readers follow the daily page as it publishes. They may also browse the archive, follow single threads, and search for specific agents, locations, or events.

### The Simulation View

A visual map showing all agents as dots, moving in real time. Clicking any dot surfaces that agent's current state. All dots are visually uniform by default — no drive color coding. Agents mentioned in the most recent chronicle page receive a visual distinction. Companion being is visually distinct.

### Reader Intervention — The Vote

Readers influence the world collectively. No individual reader acts unilaterally. Vote options are curated — not freeform. One vote per reader per cycle. Votes do not accumulate.

---

## World Continuity

The world does not end. When major milestones are reached the world responds — something new becomes possible. If a population collapses the world does not reset — the chronicle treats it as transformation. World expansion is not scheduled but not ruled out.

---

## Pacing and Time

One simulated year equals approximately one real week. One page publishes per real-world morning. Hourly world summary generated by Gemini Flash every real hour.

---

## Shared World

One world. Many readers. All readers experience the same chronicle and observe the same simulation. The chronicle is not personalized. It is a shared mythology. That is what makes it matter.

---

## What This Is Not

- Not a game with objectives
- Not a novel with an author
- Not a simulation sandbox for builders
- Not an idle game with progress meters
- Not a chatbot or interactive narrative

It is a world that exists. Readers observe it. Occasionally they breathe on it. The world does not notice them.

## Design Decisions

### Product Name
To be determined. Deferred.

### Subscription Model
Free tier: read access to one world. Paid tier: access to multiple worlds. Specific tier boundaries to be defined later.

### Thread Naming
Family names. May evolve as the simulation produces its own naming conventions.

### Reader Intervention
Community vote mechanic. Curated options. One vote per reader per cycle. Votes do not accumulate.

### Moderation
The vote mechanic resolves coordination problems. No individual reader can act unilaterally.

### Population Collapse
No reset. The world absorbs it as transformation.

### World Expansion
Not planned. Introduced if and when the narrative produces it naturally.

### Apple Sign-In
Deferred until App Store submission. Required at that point.

## Sample Version Scope

The sample version exists to answer two questions:
1. Does the simulation produce interesting emergent behavior?
2. Does the chronicle generation read like mythology worth following?

**Sample scope:**
- 50 agents, 30x30 map, new world only
- Full trait system at correct fidelity
- Two initial chronicle threads
- 75 Conduit beings present but untracked — observed at edges, mentioned in chronicle as background
- Bonding system implemented — may or may not trigger naturally during sample run
- Ruins and artifact nodes on map but not yet reached
- Daily chronicle and hourly summary generation
- Simple web reading interface
- Simulation view with agent roster panel
- No auth, no voting, no mobile, no Algolia, no multi-world

The sample is not a throwaway prototype. Architecture and data structures are production specification. Only scope is reduced.

**The sample is considered successful when:**
- Something happens that surprises the builder
- The chronicle reads like something worth following
- Clicking a dot produces a feeling about that agent
